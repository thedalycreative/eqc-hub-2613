Subject 1 · Clients & Concepts

S1.a01 · Originate and Develop Concepts

Not yet run in class · Model answers are drafted below.

The brief

You've been handed a client brief: a small local business (in class we'll pick one — a café, a plumber, a personal trainer). Turn that brief into a real design concept: moodboard, colour palette, typography choices, and a first-pass wireframe of the homepage.

This assessment is about the thinking that happens before you touch code — showing you can read a brief, ask the right questions, and turn a rough idea into something a client could sign off on.

Tasks

  1. Summarise the client's business and audience in 3–5 sentences.
  2. List 5 questions you would ask the client before starting.
  3. Assemble a moodboard (6–9 images) and explain each choice in one line.
  4. Propose a 3–5 colour palette with hex codes and a short rationale.
  5. Pick two typefaces (heading + body) from Google Fonts and justify the pairing.
  6. Sketch a wireframe of the homepage (paper, Excalidraw or Figma — any fidelity).

Model answers

Reword, don't copy. These are model answers written to show the level of detail — plagiarism software will catch a direct paste. Rewrite every answer in your own words before submitting.

Q1 · Business summary

Sample client — Bloom & Bean, a small suburban café that opens for breakfast and brunch only. Bloom & Bean is a family-run café in a residential pocket of the inner suburbs. Their audience is local — parents on the school run, remote workers looking for a mid-morning break, weekend brunch crowds. They're not competing on price; they're competing on warmth, dietary options, and consistency.

Q2 · Five questions for the client

  1. Who do you most want the website to reach — new customers, existing regulars, or catering enquiries?
  2. What single action do you most want a visitor to take on the site?
  3. Do you sell or take bookings online now, or is this the first version?
  4. Are there any brand assets already — logo, colours, photos — or do we start from scratch?
  5. What websites do you like, and what about them do you want to steal?

Q3 · Moodboard notes

Six images grouped in three pairs: Warmth (soft morning light through a window, a mug on a wooden counter), Community (small hands passing a plate, a parent chatting to staff), Craft (a hand pouring milk, close-up of a pastry). The story is "we know your name, and we know how you take your coffee".

Q4 · Colour palette

  • #2E1F1B — dark espresso brown, for headings and nav.
  • #F4EDE4 — warm parchment, for backgrounds.
  • #C08A5A — caramel, the "call-to-action" colour.
  • #3D5A3E — deep leaf green, a quiet accent for icons and borders.

Rationale: browns and creams evoke coffee and bread without cliché; the caramel picks up the pastry palette and gives buttons enough contrast to be found; the green earths the whole thing so it doesn't drift beige.

Q5 · Typography pairing

Headings: Playfair Display (serif, warm, editorial). Body: Inter (sans-serif, high legibility at small sizes). The pairing gives the site a magazine feel without making the body copy hard to read on phones — where 80% of visits will come from.

Q6 · Wireframe notes

Homepage top-to-bottom: sticky nav with the phone number and "Book a table" button → hero (photo of the counter at 8am, big headline "Neighbourhood mornings, since 2019") → opening hours strip → today's specials (3 cards) → what people are saying (2 quotes) → footer with map, socials, dietary FAQ. Nothing below the fold that a first-time visitor genuinely needs — the top of the page has to earn the scroll.
